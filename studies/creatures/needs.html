Animals: what a complete system needs

  • 14 Nov 2026
Where animals come from
  • Each kind of ground has enough kinds of animal to feel full and balanced, with predators paired with their prey.
  • Animals arrive by themselves when the land suits them, and their numbers rise and fall without help.
  • Parents have babies: small, with bigger heads, following a parent about.
  • A baby is never killed. Harm only stuns, singes or chills it.
How they look
  • Simple, cheerful, low-polygon animals, cute first. Faceted shapes with crisp angles, never blobby, and never heavy on detail.
  • A body always stays whole, with every edge joined, whatever happens to it.
  • Small dark eyes that blink now and then, and go heavy when resting or stunned.
  • The fox has big upright ears with dark tips, a bushy tail carried low with a white tip, black stockings, and a white chest, cheeks and muzzle. Its head is a little big, which makes it cute.
  • The songbird is round, with a pale breast, short legs with toes, a forked tail and wings that fold down its sides.
  • The rabbit keeps its plain round original look, which is cuter than a detailed one.
  • Babies, snow hares and other variants are the same animal with a different size or colour.
How they move
  • No animal slides. Legs step, knees bend and paws lift. Heads, ears and tails move on their own.
  • Paths curve. Animals speed up, slow down and turn at a natural rate, not on the spot.
  • Hoppers jump in real arcs. The body stretches at take-off and squashes on landing.
  • The fox trots, gallops when it runs away, and its tail bends and whips. Lying down, it curls its tail round itself.
  • Birds flap from tree to tree, land on their feet, and fold their wings when perched or on the ground.
  • Fish dart and glide, and their whole body bends as they swim.
How they live
  • Each kind has small habits of its own. Rabbits nibble and sit up to look round, the fox sniffs, pounces and play-bows, the deer grazes and stands alert, birds peck and look about, fish nibble at the surface and leap, and frogs croak and catch flies.
  • Animals rest by lying down, breathing slowly.
  • Rabbits and deer eat the grass and flowers.
  • The player can feed an animal.
Harm, death and what is left
  • Harm and death are cute and never gory.
  • Lightning shows the animal's skeleton in one flash, then it falls.
  • Near lightning, an animal is stunned. Stars circle its head and it wobbles, then shakes itself and goes.
  • Near fire, an animal is singed. Its back goes sooty and its tail smokes as it runs, and the soot fades.
  • In fire, an animal is burnt black and topples.
  • In cold, an animal frosts over, shivers and slows. Left in it, it is sealed in ice. Thawed in time, it shakes off and walks away. Too late, and it is dead.
  • Each kind meets cold its own way. The snow hare loves it, the fox runs, the deer walks off, the songbird fluffs up and flies away, the frog sleeps in the mud, and fish go deeper.
  • A fish out of water hops for the nearest water, whatever kind it is. It has only a few hops, each shorter than the last, and greys as it tires. Too far, and it dies trying.
  • A dead fish turns a dark colour, somewhere between navy, slate and near-black, so no two quite match. It floats up on its side.
  • A dead land animal lies on its side. A dead songbird lies on its back with its feet in the air.
  • A land animal dropped in deep water paddles for the shore with its head up, and may drown if it is far out.
  • After death, the body becomes a skeleton. The bones loosen a little, settle a few times with tiny jolts, and sink quietly into the ground.
  • A skeleton in water sinks straight to the bottom and lies there until it is gone.
  • Where bones go under, a small flower comes up.
What they do to the rest of the world
  • Predators hunt their prey: the fox chases rabbits, mice and birds.
  • A deeper lake supports bigger fish, which draw birds.
  • Fallout from a huge blast makes the creatures glow.
  • Lightning on water reaches everything in it.
  • Animals sense fire and lava and flee them, and a bird leaves a burning tree.
  • The remains of the dead feed the ground.
How they answer a poke and each tool
  • A poke gets a reaction in each animal's own character. A rabbit does a twisting jump, the fox play-bows then dashes off, birds leave their tree, and fish dart away.
  • The hand picks an animal up, carries it, and sets it down. A carried animal kicks or flaps.
  • A flick of the hand throws an animal in an arc. It tumbles, lands with a squash and a puff of dust, rights itself and shakes off.
  • A thrown bird catches itself in the air and flies off. A fish thrown into water swims away. A frozen block can be thrown too.
  • Setting an animal down somewhere can start a discovery.
  • Poking a body turns it to bones, and poking bones starts the crumble.
  • Warmth, fire, lightning or the pond frees an animal from ice.
What they sound like
  • Each kind has its own small voices: a fox's yip, a frog's croak and boing, birdsong, a splash, a hop.
  • Death and the crumble are silent.
  • World sounds can be turned down separately from event sounds.
Tried and turned down
  • Animals sliding in straight lines and tilting as one rigid piece: stiff.
  • A fish that dies upright, or floats belly-up: it still looks alive.
  • A skeleton that floats: bones should sink.
  • A skeleton that just vanishes: it should break a little and sink.
  • Stars, dust puffs, sparkles and rattles as the bones crumble: they drew too much attention.
  • A skeleton showing for long, or more than once, at a lightning strike: one flash is enough.
  • A body bursting apart into separate faces, like spiky fur: the gaps looked wrong.
  • A rabbit with big feet, front paws and detailed ears: a step forward, but less cute than the plain one.
  • A fox with four straight post legs and a plain stick tail: it was stiff.
  • A songbird whose folded wing is one flat blade swept back: it read as a paper plane on the ground.
