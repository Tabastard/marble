Strikes: what a complete system needs

  • 19 Nov 2026
How a strike arrives
  • A strike is an arrival carrying a payload: a rock from the sky, a storm or a beam, bringing force, heat, cold or water.
  • Strength comes from holding: a quick click is the smallest, a long hold the biggest. Bigger and stronger are the same thing.
  • Every arrival gives a moment of warning: a streak across the sky, a cloud gathering, a beam switching on.
  • A ring on the land shows where it will land and grows as it charges, with a second faint ring for how far the blast will reach.
  • Every strike can land anywhere in the bubble, right up against the glass.
  • A hit against the glass shows in cross-section at the wall, crater, layers and lake together.
  • Rocks come in on a slant from the glass, all from the same side of the sky.
The sky rocks
  • The Asteroid runs from a fast pebble on a click to a giant at full charge. The giant is not a tool of its own.
  • A giant reshapes a region and flattens forest far around it. It never reshapes the whole world.
  • Five readable steps (pebble, small, medium, large, giant), each plainly bigger than the last when seen side by side.
  • A small one leaves a rimmed crater with rare material in the rubble.
  • Deep water swallows the small ones: a splash, a ring, a dent in the bed, no crater.
  • A Meteor shower is a dozen or more small rocks over a few seconds, leaving little craters and scattered fires.
  • Wet ground keeps a shower's fires small.
  • An Ice comet brings water and cold and shatters on impact.
  • It leaves a frozen lake that melts into a real one, with snow around it. Heat melts it quickly.
  • A Fireball bursts in the air and leaves no crater.
  • It leaves a wide ring of fire, then fertile ash. Rain and wet ground put it out.
  • All four rocks are one arrival and one impact, and differ only in their numbers.
Lightning
  • A single strike, apart from the storm: click and shoot one bolt.
  • A quick click is a mini bolt, and charging it up gives a super strike.
  • A mini bolt hits one thing precisely. A super bolt reaches further and forks.
  • It sets plants alight, splits boulders, scorches the ground and kills fish in water. Wet plants do not catch.
  • It leaves glass where it hits sand.
  • On rock, a mini bolt cracks it and a super bolt shatters it to rubble.
  • On sand, a mini bolt leaves a small glass streak and a super bolt a fulgurite, a branching glass root.
  • On a tree, a mini bolt scorches it and a super bolt splits it and lights it if it is dry.
  • On water, a mini bolt stuns the fish near it, which float up and then swim off, and a super bolt's charge runs through the lake and kills them.
  • On ice, it cracks a hole or shatters the lid.
  • The bolt drops from a storm cloud that gathers for a moment first and thins away after.
  • A bolt can charge a stone or a crystal, or break it down. the designer asked (not built: this study has no crystals)
The Laser
  • Drag a thin beam that cuts a glowing trench, fuses the floor to glass, boils water and starts fires.
  • It is the precise one: it can carve a canal.
  • Deep water takes the heat.
  • A slow drag cuts deeper than a fast one. Proposed (`Tools-v2.md` suggests drag speed could set depth)
  • Its width and its heat are set apart.
  • A trench across a stream turns the stream.
  • Rock cuts more slowly than soil.
  • Held long enough in one place it bores down to the heat.
The moment of impact
  • A strike is an event the player can read at once: quick, visible, audible, exaggerated.
  • A flash that lights the land and the glass, in the payload's colour.
  • Rubble thrown out, which bounces off the glass and comes to rest.
  • A shockwave ring that runs out over the ground and the water, and fells trees as it passes.
  • A short shake that scales with strength.
  • A slow beat on the biggest hits, slowing only what is inside the sphere.
  • A dust cloud that clears over about half a minute.
  • A big hit is a moment, not a lasting change to the sky or the glass. the designer asked (rewards are moments)
What a strike leaves
  • Every strike leaves an aftermath that stays where it is until something changes it.
  • A crater is a lasting scar: a grey bowl with a rim and rubble, still there when the grass around it is green again.
  • Forest felled by a blast lies as trunks pointing away from the hit, for minutes.
  • Fresh rock on a hot crater floor sets dark and turns lighter slowly. the designer asked (as for lava rock)
  • What a strike leaves depends on the ground it hits.
  • An Asteroid on sand leaves green impact glass.
  • A giant on burned ground leaves a diamond deep in the crater.
  • Rare material is a piece lying in the world, never something in a menu.
  • A glass-lined cut where the Laser ran.
  • Burned ground is ash, which is fertile: plants come back on it fastest.
  • The world heals without help: char fades, grass and saplings return.
Fire from a strike
  • Fire is a bounded event: it flares, travels a limited distance and burns out.
  • Burned ground cannot catch again until it has properly regrown, which takes minutes.
  • Ground fire is a textured ring of light painted into the ground, tightening to its edge and burning out there, with soft embers rising. the designer liked (the nuke study, 2026-11-18)
  • The burnout shows, not only the peak, and no pale ring is left after.
  • Rain stops fire decisively.
  • A burning tree darkens and shrinks to a charred pole, with the light in the ground under it.
  • Charred trees stand for a while, fall, and saplings come up where they lay.
Chains a strike starts
  • One action sets off reactions that cross systems, each named as it happens.
  • A crater collects rain into a crater lake. the designer asked (`Reactions.md`: his example ends "Rain fills the pit and leaves a lake")
  • A giant's basin fills as a lake by itself, from the ground.
  • A hit that reaches the heat leaves a lava field.
  • A hot crater that meets water steams, and its floor sets hard.
  • Water that reaches lava sets it as black glass, once, with steam. the designer asked (lava NEEDS)
  • A hit on a slope starts a slide, and trees on it fall.
  • Fire from a strike spreads through a forest and stops.
  • A hard hit on water throws a crown of spray, sends a ring wave up the shore and deepens the lake. the designer asked (water NEEDS)
  • A hit on ice shatters it.
  • Shallow water under a burst boils away as steam.
  • Small fry hatch again in deep water after the fish are killed. the designer asked (water NEEDS: fish come back after a disaster)
What it sounds like
  • Sound is a core system: an arrival, an impact and an aftermath for every strike.
  • The arrival gives the base sound and the payload the tone, so the four sky rocks share one whistle and one impact at different pitches.
  • A falling whistle, a thump that drops, a crack, a rumble for the big ones, and pieces pattering down.
  • Ice rings as it shatters, a burst in the air is a whoomp with no thump, a hit in deep water is a splash.
  • Thunder is a crack and then a boom that rolls for a super bolt.
  • The beam hums for as long as it is on.
  • World sounds can be turned down apart from event sounds.
For judging
  • The same strike on every surface at once, so only the surface differs. the designer asked (the brief's bench)
  • The same strike at every size, side by side. the designer asked (the brief's ladder)
  • The last strike again, slowed, from the same start with the same dice.
  • Every size as numbers that can be changed while playing and handed on as text.
  • It runs cool, and a giant does not stall the frame.
Tried and turned down
  • By the designer, in earlier work:
  • Flame sprites for ground fire, as round dots, solid tongues or blended tongues: he settled on no sprites at all.
  • Anything dotted, hard-edged or pixel-square at a distance.
  • A fade that leaves a pale ghost ring.
  • Burned ground that can catch fire again after a few seconds: one spark burned for minutes.
  • A rain cloud too small to stop a fire.
  • A tool that cannot reach the land near the glass.
  • Rewards that stay on screen for good.
  • The giant meteor as a tool of its own: it is a fully charged Asteroid.
  • Five blasts that all just leave a pit: each needs its own job.
  • Lightning only as part of a storm: he wants the single bolt too.
  • Water that keeps boiling and refilling at an open well: it flickered.
  • Mist on the inside of the glass.
  • By the builder of this study, while judging stills. The designer has not seen these:
  • Burning trees as bright orange puffs: a forest alight became a field of dots.
  • Dust as dark, heavy puffs: it read as smoke and hid the crater it came from.
  • The ladder's five craters in one straight row: their burns ran together into one scar.
  • A water table close under the ground: every medium crater became a pond, and the giant's lake was nothing special.
  • Forest falling to only twice the crater's radius: the crater ate most of it.
  • Felled trees drawn as tilted crowns: from above they looked the same as standing ones.
  • Loose ground that slumps only north, south, east and west: craters and lakes settled into squares.
  • Rubble that grasses over in a few minutes: at ten minutes a giant's crater had gone green.
  • A super bolt's flash at full strength on eight strips at once: it whited out the land.
Not settled
  • The five sizes. Every number in the Asteroid's steps is a guess from stills.
  • Whether one giant on level ground should reach the heat. As left it does not. It takes low ground or a second hit.
  • How long a lava field stays open. Here, water that can reach it seals it under black glass in under half a minute.
  • How long a giant takes to charge. The frame's 1.25 s gives each step 0.3 s. Nobody has held it.
  • Whether the shake reads as a hit when the world moves inside the glass and the camera does not.
  • Whether the slow beat belongs, and on which sizes.
  • What the shockwave looks like. It has only been seen in stills.
  • How burning trees should look, given that flame sprites are out.
  • What every sound is like. None has been heard.
  • What rare material does once it lies in the rubble, and whether the diamond's recipe is the right one.
  • Types of asteroid (iron, salt, crystal and the rest of idea 19). Only payloads were built, not kinds of rock.
  • Charged things: a bolt charging a crystal, obsidian or iron ore, and water that stays charged.
  • Whether Lightning and the Laser can become rows of data like the sky rocks, which needs charge as a value on cells.
  • How far a trench must run to turn a stream for good, and whether a stream should cut its own new bed.
  • Whether a crater should ever heal. Here rubble stays bare for most of an hour.
  • What a strike does to animals. This study has none but a few fish.
  • A hit big enough to flood a lake over its banks.
  • Bullet, Hot rock, Seed rock, Crystal rock and Ash rock, the other sky rocks in `Tools-v2.md`.
