Draft — raymarched analytic volume

There is no fluid simulation and no smoke particles. The entire cloud is a closed-form density field, a pure function of (position, time, seed, settings), evaluated per pixel by a ray marcher in a WebGL2 fragment shader. Because nothing is integrated frame to frame, the timeline can be scrubbed to any instant, frozen and tuned in place, and a seed always replays identically.

Controls

Drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom, shift-drag or right-drag to pan. Space or the Nuke button detonates, P plays or pauses, R resets, 1–4 pick a camera, C makes a contact sheet, N opens these notes, H hides the UI, F toggles fullscreen. On the timeline, drag the ruler to scrub, drag a band to move it and drag either end to change its length. Drag the strip along the top of the timeline or the settings panel to move it, drag any edge or corner to resize it, and double-click the strip to dock it again. Drag the Nuke button to move it, and point at its yield icon to slide out the yield slider.

How the smoke is made

The head is a signed-distance shape (a torus smoothly joined to a dome; it starts as the fireball sphere and morphs), the stem a flared column, the dust a ground ring behind the shock front plus a low surge bank. Each shape gives a 0–1 "depth inside" value which is carved by a tiling 3D Perlin-Worley texture, so billows stay round and cauliflower-like, with a finer Worley octave eroding the edges. The toroidal roll is done by rotating the noise lookup around the vortex-ring core in the poloidal plane: material appears to rise through the middle, curl over the top and roll down the outside. Near the axis it cross-fades to an upward-scrolling lookup to avoid a pinch. Wind is a height-dependent shear applied to sample positions; dissipation raises the erosion threshold until the shape breaks into wisps.

Lighting per sample: a few exponentially spaced density taps toward the sun (self-shadow), one tap upward (sky visibility), an analytic cap-over-stem shadow, a forward-scattering phase term and a noise-driven fold darkening. Rendered at reduced resolution against the scene depth buffer, composited, then bloom and filmic tonemap.

Glow and lightning

Temperature is another analytic field: hot deep inside the shape, cooler at the surface, so the dense outer billows hide the interior and the heat shows through the cracks between them. It maps through a white → yellow → orange → red → none ramp as emission. Residual heat lives on in the vortex core and the underside of the cap for the glow duration. A point light at the core also scatters that colour into the surrounding smoke and onto the ground and trees. Lightning is a seeded schedule of short flickering point lights placed inside the vortex tube and added to the in-scattering term, so the flash lights the volume from within.

Ground fire

The fire uses a pixel fire study's spread rules: each cell lights its neighbours by chance, burns its fuel and cools. They run once when the page loads, from a closed ring around the crater, and each cell keeps when it lit and how long it burned. The ground shader reads those times, so the fire scrubs and replays exactly. It draws a textured ring of light on the land that tightens to the outer edge and goes out there, with embers made the same way as the cap's but rising.

What it does well

Soft, deep, properly volumetric cloud with real self-shadowing; exact scrubbing and determinism; one shader, cost bounded by pixels rather than particle count; every parameter is live.

Where it struggles

The shapes are authored, not simulated, so there is no true entrainment, no secondary vortices, and noise is advected rigidly so it never tears or stretches. Stem-to-cap blending is a union rather than a flow. Lights inside the cloud are unshadowed. Thin features cost steps; very low step counts band, hidden with dither. Embers are ballistic points that only approximate occlusion by the cloud.

What I would try next

Temporal reprojection to afford more steps and light taps; a curl-noise flow map for the cap so detail shears; a coarse baked light volume so glow and lightning are shadowed; a branching emissive lightning bolt; screen-space refraction on the shock front; a froxel fog pass so dust catches tree shadows.
