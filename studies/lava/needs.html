Lava and volcanoes: what a complete system needs

  • 14 Nov 2026
Where lava comes from
  • Fierce heat glows at the very bottom of the world, seen in cross-section through the glass.
  • That deep lava visibly turns and flows, slowly, so it looks alive and not painted on.
  • Digging deep enough breaks through into it and opens a well of lava with a moving surface you can look down into.
  • A vent can be opened anywhere on the land, and lava wells up out of it.
How it moves
  • Lava is thick and heavy and moves slowly. It is never thin, shiny or soupy.
  • Its surface visibly slides in the direction it is flowing.
  • It follows the land: down gullies and valleys, round bumps, filling a hollow and spilling over its lowest side.
  • It reaches down the side of a hill. It does not stall on top.
  • It runs in tongues and streams, not as one sheet that covers everything.
  • A flow is an event: it runs, slows, crusts and stops, and the world is left changed.
  • A channel dug in front of a flow, or a bank raised across it, steers it.
  • A flow that reaches an open well pours back into the deep lava.
How it cools and what it leaves
  • As it cools, a dark crust forms with glowing cracks, and the cracks show in the rock after it sets.
  • Lava sets as a thick layer of new ground that raises the land where it ran.
  • New rock sets dark and slowly turns a lighter grey over several minutes.
  • Lava that sets under rain becomes rough, pitted lava stone that looks different from smooth cracked rock.
  • Lava that meets a lake sets as black glass.
  • Over a long time, rain and weather wear new rock back into soil, and plants return.
What a volcano is, how it grows and dies
  • Clicking the same spot again grows a volcano step by step, from a small dribble to a full eruption.
  • The ground rises into a cone, and each step makes it taller and the eruption longer.
  • Only the tip of the cone darkens to bare volcanic rock. The rest of the mountain darkens only where lava has run over it.
  • The top keeps a clear, modest crater during and after the eruption. Lava fills it and spills over the rim, and the cone never sets as a sharp point.
  • The crater is deep enough to stay a clear scar after the volcano sets.
  • Where lava comes out at the top, it heaves and bubbles up, so it reads as lava coming out of the ground and then flowing down.
  • After an eruption the crater is the last thing to cool: lava down the slopes sets first, while a lake in the crater keeps glowing.
  • Sparks fly from the crater, more and bigger as the volcano grows.
  • A big volcano throws glowing embers and small boulders past the cone. Embers glow and go out, and boulders cool into dark stones that stay.
  • Nothing it throws turns into new lava or spreads where it lands.
  • A boulder rolls a little way downhill when it lands.
  • An eruption ends by itself. The cone and crater stay, and the volcano can be woken again later.
  • A volcano can be sealed or smothered, for example by rain or by filling its crater, and goes quiet for good.
What it does to water, plants, animals and the ground
  • Where lava meets water there is a hiss, a burst of steam and a ring on the water, once. Nothing keeps flickering or pulsing.
  • Water flooding an open well seals it once into a black plug, and a warm spring stays above it.
  • Lava next to a lake steams the shore but does not boil the lake dry.
  • A volcano on an island builds a black-glass shore where its lava reaches the water.
  • Lava and hot embers set the grass beside them on fire.
  • A fire spreads a short way, burns out, and leaves charred ground that regrows. Burned ground cannot burn again until it has grown back.
  • Animals sense heat and flee lava and fire. Those caught are harmed, and the ones that survive show it.
  • Fish leave water that lava has warmed.
How it answers a poke and each tool
  • Poking lava makes it give and ripple, breaking the crust so brighter lava shows.
  • Poking the deep lava at the glass, or a well, makes it heave.
  • Pouring water on lava sets it as black glass with steam.
  • Rain hardens lava into lava stone, crusts a well shut, and puts fires out at once.
  • Chilling a spot crusts lava over and seals an open well.
  • Digging scoops molten lava away and can open a new well.
  • Lava melts or swallows things dropped into it, and some precious things change in it.
What it sounds like
  • A low rumble while a volcano erupts, deeper and louder as it grows.
  • Lava bubbling and plopping, a hiss where it meets water or rain, a thud when a vent opens, a thump when a boulder lands, crackle from fires.
  • World sounds can be turned down separately from event sounds.
Tried and turned down
  • Smoke at a running vent and haze over a well: it looked wrong.
  • Dark specks floating on the lava: they looked like flies in soup.
  • Thin, fast, glossy lava: it read as liquid chrome or soup, not rock.
  • A flow that breaks into many fast little pockets: it looked like scattered drops, not one flow.
  • A flow that covers everything in its path: it was too all-consuming.
  • A flow that gets stuck on top of the hill it came from: lava has to get down the hill.
  • The whole cone turning black as it grows: only the tip and the lava's paths should darken.
  • A cone that dries to a sharp point: a volcano needs a crater.
  • Thrown pieces that melt into new lava and spread from where they land: they are embers and stones, not more lava.
  • New rock that sets pale grey straight away, or turns grey too soon: it should start dark and grey slowly.
  • Only a few strengths of vent: he wants a long range, from a dribble to a full eruption.
  • Burned ground that can catch fire again after a few seconds: one spark burned for minutes in an earlier round.
  • A perfectly smooth cone: lava fanned out over it in a sheet. The land needs gullies for lava to follow.
  • Water that keeps boiling and refilling at an open well: it flickered.
Not settled

  • How the deep lava should look as it turns.
  • What replaces smoke at a running vent, if anything.
  • How much of the land a full eruption should burn.
  • What every sound is like. None has been heard and judged.
  • Whether rain makes lava stone or black glass where rain water pools.
  • What happens when gold or oil goes into lava.
  • What happens when someone bores down a volcano's throat to the deep lava and drops something in.
  • Lava tubes and caves left by lava.
  • Whether a volcano can die, go dormant or be woken again.
  • What animals do near lava and fire.
  • Whether thrown stones can be picked up and moved.
