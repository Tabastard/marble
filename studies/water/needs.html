Water study · What it needs

Water: what a complete system needs

  • 14 Nov 2026
  • 31 asked · 3 liked · 18 proposed

Each line is something the player sees or can do. Asked the designer asked for it. Liked approved on seeing it. Proposed an idea from the study, not judged yet.

Where water comes from
  • The world starts with water of every kind: deep lakes against the glass, a broad shallow lake with beaches and islands, a pool on a high mountain top and a tarn in a crater. Asked
  • The player can bring rain clouds: soft drizzle, steady rain or a dark thunderstorm with lightning, each with its own look. Asked
  • Clouds sit high and can reach every part of the land, the rim included. Asked
  • Hot springs and geysers are lasting sources that come from heat below. Asked
  • The player can pour water anywhere, a little or a lot. Proposed
  • Water is never lost. Boiled water rises as steam, gathers into a cloud over the place it left, and rains back there. Only soaking into dry ground takes it out. Proposed
How it moves and settles
  • A lake settles within a few seconds after any disturbance, goes glassy, and its level is perfectly flat. Liked
  • Poured water runs downhill, finds the valleys, fills hollows, spills over their lowest side and reaches the lake below. Asked
  • Running water gathers into narrow streams that wind, split and join. It never slides down as one broad sheet. Asked
  • A stream wears a faint bed that draws it, and the bed fills back in once the water stops. A pour leaves no scar. Proposed
  • Water dropping off a steep edge becomes a waterfall on its own, wherever the land makes one: white streaks down the face, a curtain of drops, churn and spray at the foot, drifting mist. Asked
  • A waterfall carves a plunge pool at its foot, deeper under a taller fall, up to a limit. A trickle carves nothing, and what was carved stays. Proposed
  • Digging a channel through a lake's rim drains it until the levels match. Raising a wall across a stream dams it. Proposed
  • The player can draw a course and water follows it. Asked
How it looks
  • All water is one clear blue. Shallow water is a light tropical blue over a pale sandy floor, and deep water is darker and hides its floor. Asked
  • Depth looks right from every angle: water seen at a slant looks deeper, as a real lake does. It never looks like a colour map laid on top. Asked
  • A stream is the same blue as the lake it runs into, never grey. Asked
  • The shore has a dark wet line, a pale sand bank that forms behind it over time, a thin foam line, and slow lapping in the shallows. Liked
  • Seen from the side through the glass, water looks like an aquarium: clear near, its own blue further off and deeper down, soft light shafts, a silvery rippled ceiling, a bright line where the surface meets the glass. Asked
  • Moving light under water is gentle and never busy, and each look under water earns its place on its own. Asked
  • Bubbles are tiny soft points, always small next to a fish, rising from springs, vents, boils and fish. Asked
  • A real rainbow shows in a waterfall's spray when the sun is behind the viewer, and comes and goes as the camera moves round. Asked
  • A waterfall can become something stranger as a discovery, for example glowing, hanging drops, or fish that climb it. Asked
  • Nothing fogs or runs down the inside of the glass. Asked
Heat and cold
  • Heat on water climbs a ladder the player can see: warm, hot, boiling, then a strained surface that heaves, ending in a flash boil that turns the whole body to steam at once. Asked
  • Water takes and loses heat slowly, deep water slowest, so a lake passes through each stage. Bare ground heats at once. Proposed
  • A deep lake over hot rock is hot only near its bottom, with a glowing murk down there, while a shallow pool over the same rock simmers right through. Asked
  • As a lake bed nears the lava, more bubbles rise and a few small vents burst now and then, never many. Asked
  • Digging through to lava under a lake is one big moment: the water boils hard, drains in, steams away, the lava crusts into a plug, and the lake settles back as a hot spring. Asked
  • A bed boiled dry is left white with salt that fades, or dissolves when water returns. Proposed
  • Cold comes in stages: a thin skin of ice creeps in from the edges, then a white cracked lid that stops the water, then ice thickening downward, then shallow water frozen to its bed. Warmth undoes it in reverse. Proposed
What it does to life, ground and weather
  • Fish set the scale: schools of small fish near the surface and a few big ones deep down. They keep to deep water, sip at the surface and now and then leap out with a splash. Asked
  • Fish flee warm water, die in hot water, slow down under ice and crowd at a hole in it, and scatter from a poke, a hit or a lightning strike. Proposed
  • Fish come back after a disaster: small fry hatch in the shallows and grow, and big fish appear where there is enough deep water. Deepening a lake brings bigger fish. Asked
  • No fish dies in a hot spring the moment a world opens. Asked
  • Plants at the edge of hot water wilt. Running water never kills the grass it crosses. Asked
  • Ground near water is moister and greener. Proposed
  • Rain soaks dry ground first and runs off only when the ground is full. Proposed
  • Water on lava sets it into black glass with a hiss of steam, and rain on an open lava well seals it into a hot spring. Asked
  • Rain puts out fire at once. Asked
  • Steam from a boil or a strike gathers into a cloud of its own. Proposed
How it answers a poke and each tool
  • Poking water sends a ring out that bounces off the shore and fades, and the fish nearby dart away. Liked
  • Each kind of water answers a poke its own way: ice cracks and opens a hole that stays until chilled again, a spring bursts with bubbles, boiling water spits steam, a waterfall throws spray. Proposed
  • A hard hit from the sky, sized from a stone to a meteor, throws a crown of spray, sends a ring wave up the shore, rains the spray back down and leaves a crater that deepens the lake. A meteor also boils the water over it and sends some up as steam. Asked
  • A hit on ice shatters it. Proposed
  • Digging deepens a lake and can break through to the lava. Raising land can make a dam or a cliff for a waterfall. Proposed
  • The heat tool climbs the heat ladder and the chill tool climbs the cold one. Proposed
  • Oil spreads on water as a drifting rainbow film that gathers on the shore and can burn on the water. Asked
  • Water can turn murky from silt, green from too much growth, or glowing from fallout, and clears slowly. Proposed
  • A precious piece can be washed down a waterfall. Asked
What it sounds like
  • A plip, a splash and a plunge; a babble from streams; a roar from waterfalls, louder for a taller fall; a simmer, a boil and a bang for the flash boil; a hiss on lava; ice creaking and cracking; bubbles popping. Proposed
  • Rain on water has its own volume, and world sounds can be turned down separately from event sounds. Asked
Tried and turned down
  • Bubbles bigger than the fish and drawn in detail: far too big.
  • Moving light on the lake bed and a similar pattern on the underside of the surface at once, with bright specks on top: too much.
  • Mist and drops on the inside of the glass from steam: he does not want the glass fogging.
  • Water moved in coarse blocks: it looked blotchy.
  • Water moved as whole lakes joined by runners: good for rivers but too wormy, and it left lasting tracks on the land.
  • A grey stream, lakes in unmatched colours, and depth colour that does not change with the viewing angle: it looked painted on.
  • Poured water sliding down a hill as a sheet the width of the pour: it should gather into streams.
  • A dark stripe of dead grass left for minutes after a pour.
  • Wind ruffling the water: too much for now.
  • Fish dying in a hot spring as soon as the world opens.
  • Raindrops landing in rows: they betrayed a grid.
  • Lava under a lake that seals and reopens over and over: it pulsed.
  • A thin film of water down a wall drawn as a clear sheen: it read as a pane of glass.
  • A hit wave made in the same ripples as a poke: it focused back into spikes and covered the lake in white facets.
  • A purple, blotchy rainbow: a real bow runs red to blue and needs a wide veil of mist to show as an arc.
Not settled

  • Most of what was built after his first look: streams, waterfalls, the rainbow, hard hits, rain, the heat and cold stages, fish coming back.
  • Whether rivers need their own handling, since the lake-and-runner way was good for them.
  • What a magical waterfall becomes beyond a rainbow.
  • Rainbows anywhere else: in a geyser's spray, a hit's splash, or the sun after a storm.
  • Oil, murky water, silt and fallout in water.
  • Other heat sources on water: a laser, focused sunlight, lava running into a lake.
  • Hail, snow and snowmelt.
  • A hit big enough to flood a lake over its banks.
  • Water under the ground: seen between layers in the cross-section, seeping out as a spring, filling caves.
  • Wind on the water.
  • How much water soaking into dry ground should take out of a closed world.
  • What every sound is like. None has been made or heard.
